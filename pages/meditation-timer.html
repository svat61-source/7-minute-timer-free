The most common meditation failure is ambition. Thirty or forty-five minutes, booked before work, abandoned on the third day, and then a general conviction that "I am bad at meditation". Almost everyone who believes this is bad at meditation is actually bad at committing to a duration that is too long.

Ten minutes is not a compromise. It is long enough to get past the settling-in discomfort and long enough that a practice exists. A ten-minute practice done daily outperforms a forty-five-minute practice done twice a month, and it is not close.

What the first few minutes actually feel like

You will spend most of the first five minutes thinking about the fact that you are not concentrating. This is not failure and it is not progress you are failing at — noticing that you have drifted is exactly the skill being trained. The practice is the returning, and the returning happens fifty or a hundred times in a ten-minute session.

Sitting in silence for ten minutes is genuinely uncomfortable for most people, and that discomfort is not a sign to stop. It is a sign you are doing something slightly unfamiliar. Reduce the duration rather than abandoning the practice entirely.

Practical setup

Same time, same place, same posture. The cue-to-routine link is doing a lot of the work, and consistency in the physical details is what makes starting automatic after a couple of weeks.

A timer is genuinely more useful here than in most other contexts, because the fear of sitting for an unbounded period is a real obstacle for beginners. Knowing it is ten minutes and not an open-ended commitment is often what makes the start bearable. A soft chime at the end is a good way to finish without a jarring startle.

Common questions

How long should I meditate as a beginner?

Ten minutes daily. Consistency matters far more than duration, and ten minutes is short enough that you will keep doing it.

Do I need a special cushion or posture?

No. Sit somewhere you can be still for ten minutes with a reasonably straight back. The posture matters far less than being in the same place each day.
