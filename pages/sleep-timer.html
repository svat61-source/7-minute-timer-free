A sleep timer is a countdown that starts at bedtime and ends when you intend to be asleep. Used well, it is a boundary rather than a deadline. The most valuable thing it does is give you a defined end to the evening, which is the thing people actually struggle with.

Most people do not have trouble falling asleep because they spent six hours on their phone. They have trouble because the last thing they did was consume unpredictable, mildly stimulating content with no natural stopping point. There is no chapter in an infinite feed, so there is no moment at which it feels finished.

How long to give yourself

Fifteen minutes of deliberate wind-down is the common recommendation and a reasonable default. Ten is enough for most nights if you are genuinely ready. An hour is for people who have a real transition ritual — a book, a stretch, a conversation. What matters is that the duration is chosen in advance and the timer enforces it.

Set the timer, put the phone somewhere you cannot reach it without standing up, and do something boring on purpose. A physical book is ideal. A paper task, a stretching routine, a low-stakes conversation with someone else — anything that is not a screen works, because the light and the content type are both doing work on your arousal level.

Why not just set a wake-up alarm

Because an alarm tells you when to start the morning. A sleep timer tells you when to stop the evening. The second one is harder and more valuable. Anyone can manage a fixed wake time; almost nobody manages a fixed wind-down, because an unbounded evening is the default and nobody interrupts it.

Consistency matters more than duration

A fixed 15-minute wind-down that you actually follow is worth considerably more than an hour-long one you abandon. The habit forms around the duration you keep. Start short enough that you never miss it, and extend only once it is genuinely automatic.

Common questions

Should I use my phone as a sleep timer?

You can, but set it then put the phone out of reach. A sleep timer that stays in your hand does not wind anything down.

Is 15 minutes enough to fall asleep?

It is enough for a wind-down, not necessarily for sleep itself. Most people need 20 to 30 minutes after getting into bed, so start your timer before you get in.
