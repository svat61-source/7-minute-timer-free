The 4-7-8 breathing technique, in two minutes

The 4-7-8 pattern is three numbers long. Breathe in through your nose for four counts. Hold for seven. Breathe out through your mouth for eight. That is one cycle, and most people do four to six of them and stop.

The reason the long exhale is the important part is physiological rather than mystical. Exhaling longer than you inhale slows your heart rate, because heart rate rises slightly on the inhale and falls on the exhale. Extending the exhale extends the falling phase. The technique is essentially a manual way to shift your autonomic state downward, and it works on that basis whether or not you find it meaningful.

Doing it without getting light-headed

Most people breathe 4-7-8 too fast at the start, and then feel woozy and conclude the technique is wrong. The counts must be slow and the breaths must be shallow enough to stay comfortable. If you are counting in your head faster than about one count per second, slow down.

Breathe through your nose for the inhale and pursed lips for the exhale, as though blowing out a candle slowly. The resistance keeps the breath from being ragged. Stopping after four cycles is plenty at the beginning; many people find that is all they need.

When it is genuinely useful

It is well suited to a short acute spike — before a difficult conversation, the first two minutes after waking, the moment you notice you have been clenching your jaw for twenty minutes. The effect is real but it is a downshift, not a solution, and it lasts for minutes rather than hours.

It is much less useful as a daily practice than as a tool for a specific moment. The daily benefit people attribute to it comes more from the ritual of stopping than from the specific ratio.

If you get light-headed

Stop, breathe normally for a minute, and return with a shorter ratio. Box breathing — four in, four held, four out, four held — has an equal exhale and is a better default for people who find 4-7-8 too aggressive. Six breaths per minute is the underlying rate that most breathing research settles on, and any pattern that lands there will do.

Common questions

How many times should I do 4-7-8 breathing?

Four cycles is a good starting point. Most people feel the effect within two minutes and there is no benefit to going much longer.

Is 4-7-8 better than box breathing?

Neither is universally better. 4-7-8 has a longer exhale and a stronger downshift, box breathing is easier to sustain and less likely to make you light-headed.
