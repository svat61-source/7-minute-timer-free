The 7-minute workout: 12 exercises in 7 minutes 50 seconds

The circuit below is the one people mean when they say "7-minute workout": twelve bodyweight exercises at 30 seconds each, separated by 10-second transitions. Set the timer to Workout mode and it runs the whole thing for you, calling out each move so you can keep your eyes off the screen.

The 12 exercises, in the order that matters

The sequence alternates muscle groups on purpose. You never hit the same group twice in a row, which is what lets twelve exercises survive inside eight minutes. The engine above reads this list directly, so the timer and the instructions can never drift apart.

  1. 1

    Jumping jacks

    Start with feet together, arms at your sides. Jump them apart and overhead at the same time. Get as many in as you can with clean form rather than fast, sloppy ones.

    Easier: step one foot out at a time and bring the arms up without jumping.

  2. 2

    Wall sit

    Back flat to a wall, feet hip-width and slightly forward. Slide down until knees are bent to roughly 90 degrees. Hold, and keep your weight in your heels.

    Easier: hold a shallower angle, or lean against a chair back with the slide reduced.

  3. 3

    Push-ups

    Plank position, hands under shoulders, body in one line. Lower until your chest is near the floor, then press back up. Hips stay level throughout.

    Easier: drop to your knees, or push against a countertop at an incline.

  4. 4

    Abdominal crunches

    Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Press your lower back into the floor and curl your shoulders toward your knees. Lower under control, do not drop.

    Easier: keep knees higher and use a smaller range of motion, or crunch one arm at a time.

  5. 5

    Step-ups onto a chair

    Face a sturdy chair or bench. Step up fully onto it, stand, step down, then repeat with the other leg. Use a chair with no wheels and keep it braced against a wall.

    Easier: use a low step instead, or skip the step entirely and alternate heel lifts.

  6. 6

    Squats

    Feet shoulder-width, hips hinge back as if reaching for a chair. Keep weight in your heels and your chest up. Stand fully at the top.

    Easier: hands on hips, sit back only halfway, or squat to a chair and tap it lightly.

  7. 7

    Triceps dips on a chair

    Sit on the front edge of a sturdy chair, hands on the edge beside you. Slide your hips off, bend elbows straight back, lower, and press up.

    Easier: keep the knees bent and shorten the dip, or use a low bench with a small range.

  8. 8

    Forearm plank

    On your forearms with elbows under shoulders, body in one straight line from head to heels. Squeeze your glutes and brace your core. Do not let the hips sag or pike up.

    Easier: forearms on a sturdy surface such as a bed frame or low bench, or drop to your knees.

  9. 9

    High knees, running in place

    Run on the spot, driving each knee up to hip height. Swing your arms as though you were running down a road rather than in place.

    Easier: march instead of run, keeping the same upright posture and arm drive.

  10. 10

    Lunges

    Step forward with one foot, dropping the back knee toward the floor. Both knees near 90 degrees, front knee above the ankle. Push back to standing and switch legs.

    Easier: hold a wall for balance, or do reverse lunges for a kinder version on the knees.

  11. 11

    Push-up and rotation

    From a push-up position, press up and rotate your torso, extending one arm straight toward the ceiling. Return, switch sides, and repeat on the other arm.

    Easier: do wide push-ups from the knees and skip the rotation, or rotate from a high plank position.

  12. 12

    Side plank

    On one forearm, legs stacked and body straight. Lift the hips until you form a line from head to feet. Fifteen seconds a side, then switch.

    Easier: drop the lower knee to the floor for support, or lift only to hip height rather than full extension.

How to use it properly

Warm up first. Two or three minutes of dynamic movement — marching, arm circles, leg swings — before the first jumping jack. Static stretching immediately before a high-intensity circuit actively reduces output.

Work at maximum for 30 seconds

Within each 30-second window, the goal is as many good repetitions as you can manage, not a target pace. The short gaps are transitions, not recovery: you are still moving between exercises, and your heart rate is meant to stay elevated right through.

How long one circuit actually is

Twelve exercises at 30 seconds is 6 minutes of work. Add the eleven 10-second transitions and you get 470 seconds — 7 minutes 50 seconds. It is called a 7-minute workout because of rounding, not because it is precisely seven. Knowing the real figure matters if you are timing circuits against something else.

Do more than one circuit

The original protocol asks for two to three circuits, so a full session is closer to 16 to 24 minutes than seven. The picker at the top of this page sets one, two or three circuits with a rest between them, which is the single biggest difference between the real routine and most web timers.

Does it actually work?

The research is broadly positive with real caveats. The protocol has been replicated across a number of trials and the broad finding is that high-intensity circuit work produces meaningful cardiovascular benefits in a very short time. The caveats: it is not equivalent to a 30-minute session, the intensity is hard to sustain, and people consistently underestimate how much they underperform in the final exercises of circuit three.

It works well as a floor rather than a ceiling. Consistency across months matters far more than any single session, and seven minutes is a small enough ask that you will actually do it on a bad day — which is precisely when a hard workout is most useful and most likely to be skipped.

Check with a doctor before starting a new exercise routine, particularly if you have joint, cardiovascular or back issues. The modifications on every exercise above exist because not every movement suits every body, and dropping the range of motion is better than dropping the session.

Common questions

How long is the 7-minute workout really?

One circuit is 470 seconds, or 7 minutes 50 seconds: 12 exercises at 30 seconds each plus 11 transitions of 10 seconds.

Do I only need to do it once?

The protocol recommends two to three circuits, so a full session is closer to 16 to 24 minutes. This timer offers 1, 2 or 3 circuits with a rest between them.

What equipment do I need?

A sturdy chair without wheels for step-ups and triceps dips, and a wall for wall sits. Nothing else. Every exercise has a lower-impact modification.

Why does the order matter?

The sequence alternates muscle groups so you never repeat a group back to back. That alternation is what makes 12 exercises tolerable in under eight minutes.
