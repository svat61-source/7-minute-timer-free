Pacing a presentation without rushing the ending

The reason talks get rushed is that the decision about what to cut is deferred until the moment you are already over. Standing in front of an audience discovering you have four slides left and six minutes is a bad way to make an editorial decision.

A visible timer changes where the decision happens. If the countdown is on the wall or the lectern, you make the cut at minute eight of a fifteen-minute slot, calmly, rather than at minute fourteen in front of everyone.

Budget the time before you build

Work out the total slot length first, then subtract five minutes of questions if there are any. For a fifteen-minute talk with questions that leaves ten minutes of talking, which is roughly 1,100 words at a measured pace of about 110 words per minute. Speaking faster than that under pressure is the main reason talks feel rushed even when the content was fine.

Divide that by your slide count and you have a per-slide budget. A title slide is five seconds. A content slide is 60 to 90 seconds. A slide you cannot summarise in one sentence is usually two slides.

Rehearse with a hard stop

Run the talk at least three times with a timer and cut it to the length, not to the content you planned. The cuts are always the same ones: the second example, the caveat, the backup slide you thought you needed. Rehearsal exists to find those cuts while there is still time to make them well.

Rehearse standing and out loud, with the actual slides. Silent reading is substantially faster than speaking, which is why a talk you rehearsed in your head runs forty percent long.

When you are running long

The recovery move is to state your remaining time and commit to a specific endpoint: "I have three minutes left, so I will cover the last two slides and stop for questions." Announcing it buys you enormous latitude, because the audience now has a shared contract and nobody is silently getting impatient.

Put your final slide up permanently in the corner. It is an escape hatch you will probably never need, and knowing it is there means you do not need it.

Common questions

How many words per minute for a presentation?

Around 110 to 130 words per minute for a normal speaking pace. Budget for 110 if you are nervous, since people speed up when they are.

What if I finish early?

It is much better than running long. Keep a backup slide or an extended example ready so you can add rather than cut, and never speak faster to fill the slot.
